# **September Highlights**

**September is National Preparedness Month**\
Each September, [FEMA’s Ready Campaign](https://www.ready.gov/stand-ready) celebrates National Preparedness Month. Since 2004, the campaign has taken the month to encourage Americans to prepare for emergencies. The month is a great time to take small steps to make a big difference in being prepared.\
Preparedness is everyone’s responsibility, and disasters and emergencies can happen at any time, anywhere, and sometimes without warning.

### **Simulated Emergency Test (SET)**

**The Simulated Emergency Test (SET) this year on October 3rd is a repeat of last year’s – because the skills are same as for a EmComm deployment! And it’s fun!**

**Rumor on social media** has it that Sasquatch, i.e., Bigfoot, will be seen in Eastern Washington the first weekend in October in state and national parks or on mountain summits. Hundreds of thousands are expected to come to capture a picture of the elusive creature during their annual migration. ARES/ACS/AuxComm groups will be called out to observe and report any sightings as well as general crowd and situational reports. Will your group be ready?

ARRL has the SET forms ready at <https://www.arrl.org/simulated-emergency-test> .

I request that you perform the following actions as part of your SET:

1. Use of Winlink – radio is preferred but Telnet is acceptable.:

   1. Send a WA State Field Situation Report to your EC and me (AG7QP)
   2. Send a message via the National Traffic System to Jo Whitney, KA7LJQ, Section Manager, and Frank Hutchison, AG7QP, Section Emergency Coordinator
2. Take pictures and share them with me ([redacted: contact detail]). If you can include Sasquatch in the pictures, that will be a bonus! Extra bonus if you use Winlink.
3. Make as many contacts as you can using the rules of POTA and/or SOTA.
4. Send me a copy of your AAR and include a list of participants.
5. Have FUN!

### The Radio Amateur's Code

The Radio Amateur is

**CONSIDERATE**...The radio amateur never knowingly operates in such a way as to lessen the pleasure of others.

**LOYAL**...The radio amateur offers loyalty, encouragement and support to other amateurs, local clubs, the IARU Radio Society in their country, through which Amateur Radio in their country is represented nationally and internationally.

**PROGRESSIVE**...The radio amateur keeps their station up to date. It is well-built and efficient. Their operating practice is above reproach.

**FRIENDLY**...The radio amateur operates slowly and patiently when requested; offers friendly advice and counsel to beginners; kind assistance, cooperation and consideration for the interests of others. These are the marks of the amateur spirit.

**BALANCED**...Radio is a hobby, never interfering with duties owed to family, job, school or community.

**PATRIOTIC**...The radio amateur's station and skills are always ready for service to country and community.

- adapted from the original Amateur's Code, written by Paul M. Segal, W9EEA, in 1928
