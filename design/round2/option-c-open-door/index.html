Joining, in four steps

Start at 0 if you’re not licensed yet, or at 1 if you are. You can stop at ARESAmateur Radio Emergency Service, the ARRL’s volunteer emergency program, open to any licensed ham., or go on to ACSAuxiliary Communications, Spokane County Emergency Management’s volunteer radio program. Members pass a county background check..

Where are you starting from?

Link to this path:

ARES A national ARRL program open to any licensed ham. Events, training and weather work need no declared emergency.

ACS The county’s own volunteer radio unit. After a background check, the county can call on you for its operations centers, hospitals and shelters.

You can join ARES only, or both. In the group’s own words: Even [if] you don’t or can’t qualify for ACS, our ARES group welcomes you.

You’re on the team. Staying current lives on the members pages: what’s due this month and this year.

Here’s what it takes

Honest numbers, from the county’s ACS task book. ARES and ACS members train side by side.

  • 1
    1 net check-in a monthWeekly is encouraged.
  • 1
    1 meeting or workshop a quarterMonthly is encouraged.
  • 1
    1 field operation a yearAn activation, an exercise or a public-service event.
  • 2
    2 kits, readyA personal kit for 24 hours or more (72 preferred) and a 2 m / 70 cm radio kit.

New members also complete four free online FEMA courses and the ARRL Basic EmComm course in their first year. The full training path

Every Tuesday

8:00 PM, the Tuesday net

  1. 7:30 PM

    The GMRS net first

    On third Tuesdays, for county volunteers with GMRS licenses.

  2. 7:55 PM

    Tune in

    Any radio or scanner that receives 147.300 MHz will do.

  3. 8:00 PM

    The net opens

    Net control (a member on the rota) opens. Officials check in, then members.

  4. 5th Tue

    Simplex first

    The net starts radio to radio, then moves to W7GBU for traffic.

  5. Then

    Visitors: this is your cue

    Licensed? Give your call sign slowly and phonetically, your name and location. Not licensed? Keep listening; it’s how everyone learns.

  6. 2nd, 4th Tue

    The Winlink assignment

    The Winlink lead gives the assignment. Have paper and pencil ready.

  7. Last

    Traffic, then the close

    Listed traffic and late check-ins, then net control closes the net.

At a glance

How long
As long as you like. The net starts at 8:00 PM and runs as long as check-ins last.
Where
From home, your car, anywhere you can hear the repeater.
What to bring
A radio or scanner that receives 147.300 MHz. Licensed? Your call sign.
License needed?
Not to listen. To check in on the air, a Technician license or higher.

What we do

One team of licensed amateur radio (“ham”) operators, ready when phones, power or the internet can’t be counted on, and busy the rest of the year.

See how it works

  • The county’s radio room and trailer

    When the county activates us, members staff its radio room and communications trailer and pass messages where normal links are down.

    How activation works

  • Hospitals

    A hospital team helps keep backup radio and computer equipment in place and working at Greater Spokane area hospitals.

    Where we work

  • PHOTO NEEDEDVolunteers at a Bloomsday aid station, wide, landscape

    Community events

    Primary or backup communications for Bloomsday, the Lilac Festival Armed Forces Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bike races.

    Public-service events

  • Weather and Winlink

    Many members are trained SKYWARN spotters for the National Weather Service in Spokane. WinlinkEmail by radio: messages and forms sent over the air, with or without the internet. sends email-style messages by radio, through the group’s own W7GBU-10 gateway.

Who we serve

Spokane County Emergency Management is our primary served agency. It activates ACS members and runs the county application, background check and ID cards.

  • National Weather Service, SpokaneSKYWARN weather spotting and severe-weather nets.
  • Greater Spokane area hospitalsBackup radio and computer equipment kept ready.
  • American Red CrossService under the national ARRL–Red Cross agreement.
  • Community event organizersBloomsday, the Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bike races.

We’re an ARRL Amateur Radio Emergency Service group, and we share a backup repeater and packet paths with the Inland Empire VHF Radio Amateurs under a written agreement.

Represent an agency or an event?

Government agencies request amateur radio support through Spokane County Emergency Management, which activates the team. Event organizers can reach our public-events coordinator.

We’re an auxiliary support function. Our members are volunteers, not first responders.
