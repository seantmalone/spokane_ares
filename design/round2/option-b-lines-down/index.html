When other systems go down, Spokane County still has a voice.

Licensed amateur radio volunteers providing backup communications for Spokane County Emergency Management, the National Weather Service, area hospitals and community events.

Join the team Follow one message through a night without phones

When normal systems are overloaded or down, our members staff the county’s radio room and communications trailer, and pass messages by voice and by radio email.

We’re volunteers, not first responders. We back up the agencies that are.

Could you be the one on the radio?

Four steps. Start at 0 if you’re not licensed yet, or at 1 if you are. You can stop at Amateur Radio Emergency Service: the ARRL’s national volunteer program, open to any licensed ham., or go on to Auxiliary Communications: Spokane County Emergency Management’s own volunteer radio program. Members pass a county background check..

Not licensed yet?

Listening needs no license. Any radio or scanner that receives 147.300 MHz picks up the Tuesday net at 8:00 PM.

  • Study for the exam on HamStudy (opens hamstudy.org)
  • SKYWARN spotter training (opens weather.gov), no license needed

Every Tuesday night we practice.

The weekly net is a directed net that doubles as emergency-operations training. Members take turns as net control, and visitors are welcome to check in after members.

For served agencies

Government agencies request amateur radio support through Spokane County Emergency Management, which activates the team. Organizers of Bloomsday, the Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bike races can reach our public-events coordinator.

Who we serve

Spokane County Emergency Management
Our primary served agency. It activates ACS members and runs the application, background check and ID cards.
National Weather Service, Spokane
SKYWARN weather spotting and severe-weather nets.
Greater Spokane area hospitals
Backup radio and computer equipment, kept ready.
American Red Cross
Service under the national ARRL and Red Cross agreement.

We’re an ARRL Amateur Radio Emergency Service group, and we share a backup repeater and packet paths with the Inland Empire VHF Radio Amateurs under a written agreement. ARES-ACS is an auxiliary support function; its members are not first responders.
