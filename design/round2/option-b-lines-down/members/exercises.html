  2. Oct3Sat

    Happening nowExercise

    Simulated Emergency Test (SET)

    Sat, Oct 3, all day.

    What
    The ARRL’s annual national exercise, on the first weekend of October. The Eastern Washington Section Emergency Coordinator sets this year’s scenario. It is lighthearted, but the skills are the same as for a real deployment.
    Your tasks
    1. Use Email by radio. Members practice it on Winlink nights, the 2nd and 4th Tuesdays. (radio preferred, Telnet acceptable) to send a WA State Field Situation Report to your EC and to the Section Emergency Coordinator (AG7QP).
    2. Send a message through the National Traffic System to the Section Manager (KA7LJQ) and the Section Emergency Coordinator (AG7QP).
    3. Take pictures of your station and operation to share.
    4. Make as many contacts as you can using the rules of POTA and/or SOTA.
    5. Send an after-action report (AAR) with a list of participants to the Section Emergency Coordinator.
    6. Have fun.
    Where
    Your station, or wherever the local plan puts you. POTA and SOTA contacts are made from parks and summits.
    Bring
    A Winlink station (radio preferred, Telnet acceptable) and a camera or phone for photos.
    Sign up
    Local plans, including where to be and who is net control, are on groups.io.
    Report to
    Your EC, and the Section Emergency Coordinator (AG7QP), who also gets the after-action report.

Before, during and after

1Before

  • Check the assignment and plan on groups.io. Know your The operator who runs a net, calling stations in turn so every message is heard. Members take turns, and new net controls are coached. and your alternate (146.880 MHz if The group’s call sign and repeater, honoring Ira “Dean” Bula. 147.300 MHz, +600 kHz offset, 100 Hz tone. is down).
  • Charge batteries, check your go-kit against the kit list, and program your handheld.
  • Carry forms: ICS-213, ICS-213RR, ICS-214 and 214a, ICS-309, ARRL Radiogram. Forms
  • Tip: fill out an ICS-213RR that describes your go-kit, and give it to whoever authorizes your deployment.

2During

  • Check in with net control when you arrive and when you leave.
  • Log every message on an ICS-309 and your activity on an ICS-214.
  • Keep transmissions short, and leave gaps for priority traffic.
  • Look after yourself and the people around you: rest, eat, drink water. Don’t hesitate to admit when things are getting to be too much.

3After

  • Send your logs and your part of the report as the exercise lead asks.
  • Leaders hold an action review within 72 hours of the end of an event.
  • For the Simulated Emergency Test: the ARRL’s annual national exercise., an after-action report with a participant list goes to the Section Emergency Coordinator.
  • Auxiliary Communications: Spokane County Emergency Management’s own volunteer radio program. Members pass a county background check. members: note the date, and get the sign-off for your task book’s yearly field operation.
