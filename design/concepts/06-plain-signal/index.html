When other systems go down,
Spokane County still has a voice.

We are licensed amateur radio (“ham”) operators who volunteer to provide backup communications when normal systems are overloaded or down.

We support Spokane County Emergency Management, the National Weather Service, local hospitals and community events.

How to join

Where are you starting from?

Pick one to put your next step first. You get a link you can read out on the net.

  1. 0

    Your next step

    Step 0: Get licensedDone

    Every operator needs an FCC amateur radio license. Local classes run through the year, and volunteer examiners test after the class. You can listen to the Tuesday net before you are licensed.

    Find a class or exam session
    How long
    A class, then an exam session. Classes are held periodically through the year.
    You need
    Study time, and the FCC’s $35 application fee.

  2. 1

    Your next step

    Step 1: Join ARESDone

    ARES, the Amateur Radio Emergency Service, is an ARRL program open to any licensed ham. Register with Spokane County ARES and start the ARRL ARES Task Book, which tracks your participation and qualifications.

    Download the registration form
    How long
    One form. Bring it to a meeting or email it in.
    You need
    A Technician class license or higher. ARRL membership is not required.

  3. 2

    Your next step

    Step 2: Get connectedDone

    Subscribe to the team’s Groups.io list for meetings and events. Check into the Tuesday net, then come to a Third Thursday meeting or a Second Saturday workshop.

    Subscribe on Groups.io
    How long
    Ongoing. Members check into the net at least once a month.
    You need
    A 2 m radio on 147.300 MHz, +600 kHz, 100 Hz tone.

  4. 3

    Your next step

    Step 3: Qualify for ACS (optional)Done

    ACS is Spokane County Emergency Management’s own volunteer communications unit. After a county background check, the county can activate you to work in emergency operations centers, incident command posts, hospitals and shelters.

    “Even [if] you don’t or can’t qualify for ACS, our ARES group welcomes you, and you can still volunteer for many events.”

    Apply to Spokane County
    How long
    Up to about a year as a New Member, working toward Radio Operator (RADO).
    You need
    A county background check, then FEMA IS-100 and IS-700 (free online courses).
    1. 3.1

      Apply to the county. Submit the Spokane County Emergency Management Volunteer Emergency Worker Application.

    2. 3.2

      Pass a background check. The county runs a security and background check.

    3. 3.3

      Receive a volunteer number. The county assigns it, and you can start taking part in a limited way.

    4. 3.4

      Attend New Member Orientation. Usually the 4th Tuesday at 18:00, followed by a comm room tour and the 20:00 net.

    5. 3.5

      Complete new-member training. The ACS Task Book asks for FEMA IS-100, IS-200, IS-700 and IS-800, the ARRL Basic EmComm course, HIPAA training and an Alert Spokane registration.

    6. 3.6

      Receive your county volunteer ID card. It is issued once IS-100, IS-700, the background check and a six-month activity period are complete.

What the team does

Spokane County ARES-ACS is one team with two sides. The two sides train and operate together.

ARES Amateur Radio Emergency Service
A national ARRL program open to any licensed ham. ARES members can support community events, the National Weather Service and the Red Cross without an emergency being declared.
ACS Auxiliary Communications
Spokane County Emergency Management’s own volunteer communications unit. Members have passed a county background check, so the county can activate them to work where sensitive information is handled.
RACES Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service
The FCC provision that lets hams operate for government during emergencies. ACS carries on the county’s RACES program.

  • Emergency communications

    Spokane County Emergency Management activates the team. Members staff the county radio room and comm trailer.

  • Hospitals

    Backup radio and computer equipment at Greater Spokane area hospitals, with a Hospital Net after the Tuesday net.

  • Public service events

    Primary or backup communications for Bloomsday, the Lilac Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bicycle races.

  • Severe weather

    SKYWARN spotting for the National Weather Service in Spokane, with nets activated in severe weather.

  • Digital messaging

    Winlink email over radio through the W7GBU-10 gateway, with regular practice assignments on ICS-213 forms.

  • Exercises

    The annual Simulated Emergency Test, joint exercises with other agencies, and monthly training.

“Amateur radio can be particularly helpful because it can function even if other modes of communication cannot.”

Eastern Washington Standing Order 1b

Who we serve

  • Spokane County Emergency ManagementPrimary served agency. Activates ACS and runs the background check and volunteer ID.
  • National Weather Service, SpokaneSKYWARN spotting and severe-weather nets.
  • Greater Spokane area hospitalsBackup communications and the Hospital Net.
  • American Red CrossService under the ARRL and Red Cross agreement, and shelter training.
  • Spokane County Sheriff’s OfficeHelicopter safety training.
  • Washington State Emergency ManagementState nets, including WSEN on 3.985 MHz.
  • Inland Empire VHF Radio AmateursFormal agreement for the 146.88 MHz backup repeater and packet and Winlink nodes.
  • Event organizersBloomsday, the Lilac Torchlight Parade and Valleyfest.

ARES is a program of ARRL, the national association for amateur radio. ACS is the volunteer communications unit of Spokane County Emergency Management.
