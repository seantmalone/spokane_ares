    Not a member yet?

    We’re licensed ham radio operators who volunteer to keep Spokane County talking when phones and the internet can’t. No emergency experience needed: new members learn at orientation, workshops and the Tuesday net.

    1. 0

      Get licensed

      FCC Technician class or higher.

      Not licensed yet?
      • Listen to the Tuesday net first. Listening needs no license.
      • Take a license class. They’re sponsored by county Emergency Management, the VHF Club and local ARES schedule to be confirmed
      • Test locally or online with GLAARG on HamStudy. The FCC charges a $35 application fee.
      • Want to help sooner? SKYWARN spotter training needs no license.
    2. 1

      Join ARES

      Fill in the registration form. Open to any licensed ham; no ARRL membership needed.

    3. 2

      Get connected

      Join the Groups.io list, check into a net, come to a meeting.

    4. 3

      Qualify for ACS (optional)

      County application and background check, so the county can call on you.

    What ARES-ACS does

    In plain English

    We’re one team of licensed amateur radio (“ham”) operators who volunteer to provide backup communications when normal systems are overloaded or down. The team has two sides.

    ARES

    Amateur Radio Emergency Service

    A national ARRL program open to any licensed ham. ARES members support community events, the National Weather Service and the Red Cross, with no declared emergency needed.

    ACS

    Auxiliary Communications

    Spokane County Emergency Management’s own volunteer radio unit, carrying on the county’s RACES program. Members pass a county background check, so they can work in emergency operations centers, command posts, hospitals and shelters.

    Join ARES only, or both. Even if you don’t or can’t qualify for ACS, our ARES group welcomes you.

    • County operationsThe radio room and comm trailer for Emergency Management.
    • HospitalsBackup radio at area hospitals, and the Hospital Net.
    • Community eventsBloomsday, the Lilac Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bike races.
    • Severe weatherSKYWARN spotting and nets for NWS Spokane.
    • Digital messagesWinlink email by radio, through the W7GBU-10 gateway.
    “Amateur radio can be particularly helpful because it can function even if other modes of communication cannot.”Eastern Washington Standing Order 1b
