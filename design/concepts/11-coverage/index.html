One team, two ways to serve

Spokane County ARES-ACS is one team of licensed amateur radio (“ham”) operators who volunteer to provide backup communications when normal systems are overloaded or down.

ACS Auxiliary Communications

Spokane County Emergency Management’s own volunteer communications unit. It carries on the county’s RACES program (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service, the FCC provision that lets hams operate for government during emergencies). ACS members pass a county background check, so the county can activate them to work inside emergency operations centers, incident command posts, hospitals and shelters.

ARES Amateur Radio Emergency Service

A national ARRL program open to any licensed ham. ARES members support community events, the National Weather Service and the Red Cross without an emergency being declared.

Spokane County the ground both sides stand on

The two sides train and operate together on the same nets. You can join ARES only, or both. Formerly known as Spokane County ARES/RACES.

What happens at each pin

Six kinds of places on the map, the work volunteers do at each one, and the role it needs.

  • Where we meet

    Spokane County DEM building, 1121 W. Gardner Ave, Spokane

    Training meetings on Third Thursdays, hands-on workshops on Second Saturdays from 9 AM to noon, and New Member Orientation on the 4th Tuesday. Members also staff the county’s radio communications room and comm trailer.

    Role it needs: everyone starts here, at orientation.

  • Public events

    Bloomsday and the Lilac Torchlight Parade, downtown Spokane

    Primary or backup communications for Bloomsday, the Lilac Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bicycle races. Operators hold posts along the route and pass what they see to net control.

    Role it needs: event operator. Open to any licensed ARES member.

  • Hospitals we support

    Greater Spokane area hospitals (shown as a general area)

    A Hospital Coordinator works with area hospitals to keep backup radio and computer equipment in place and working. A Hospital Net follows the regular Tuesday net on some weeks.

    Role it needs: hospital team, for ACS members with HIPAA training.

  • Repeater W7GBU

    147.300 MHz, +600 kHz offset, 100 Hz tone

    Home of the Tuesday 8 PM net, where members take turns as net control. The call sign is a memorial to Ira “Dean” Bula, who started the morning round table still known as the 0730 Bunch.

    Role it needs: net control station. The Net Manager coaches new net controls.

  • NWS Spokane

    SKYWARN weather spotting

    Many members are trained SKYWARN spotters for the National Weather Service Spokane office, and nets are activated in severe weather. Spotting is encouraged, not required.

    Role it needs: weather spotter. You can take the training before you are licensed.

  • Field stations

    Wherever an exercise or event needs one

    Members bring their own go-kits: a station that can run for at least 72 hours on 2 m and 70 cm, and often HF and Winlink for digital messages through the group’s W7GBU-10 gateway.

    Role it needs: field or Winlink operator.

Hospital stations, equipment caches and staging sites are not on the public map. Members see them after signing in.

Your route onto the team

Three steps get you on the air with us. A fourth, optional step lets the county call on you. Not licensed yet? Start at zero.

  1. STEP0

    Get licensed

    You need an FCC Technician license or higher. Local classes run through the year, and exams are offered locally and online.

    Not licensed yet? Start here

  2. STEP1

    Join ARES

    Fill out the Spokane County ARES registration form and bring it to a meeting or email it in. ARRL membership is not required.

    Download the registration formGet the ARRL ARES Task Book

  3. STEP2

    Get connected

    Subscribe to the Groups.io list, check into the Tuesday net, and come to a Third Thursday meeting or a Second Saturday workshop.

    Subscribe on Groups.ioHow to check in Tuesday

  4. STEP3

    Qualify for ACS Optional

    • Apply as a county Volunteer Emergency Worker
    • Pass the background check and get a volunteer number
    • Attend New Member Orientation (4th Tuesday)
    • Complete IS-100, IS-200, IS-700, IS-800 and ARRL Basic EmComm

    County volunteer application

Not licensed yet?

You can start before you hold a license. Here’s the path people in Spokane County take.

  1. Listen in. Tune to the Tuesday 8 PM net on 147.300 MHz, or come to a Third Thursday meeting or Second Saturday workshop.

  2. Take a license class. Classes run periodically through the year, sponsored by Spokane County Emergency Management, the VHF Club and local ARES-RACES.

  3. Take the exam. Local volunteer examiner sessions, or online through GLAARG on HamStudy. The FCC charges a $35 application fee.

  4. Try SKYWARN meanwhile. NWS spotter training is online or in person, and needs no license. Spotter training

Who we serve

The agencies and organizations the team trains with and supports, and what each relationship involves.

Spokane County Emergency Management

Primary served agency. The county activates ACS, runs the background check and issues volunteer IDs.

Seal shown as an affiliation mark, pending the county’s permission.

Emblem is ARRL’s trademark, shown pending permission.
