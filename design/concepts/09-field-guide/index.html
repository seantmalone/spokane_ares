What the team does

Spokane County ARES-ACS is one team of licensed amateur radio ("ham") operators who volunteer to provide backup communications when normal systems are overloaded or down. The team has two sides.

ARES

Amateur Radio Emergency Service

A national ARRL program, open to any licensed ham.

Field marks

  • No background check needed
  • Supports community events, the National Weather Service and the Red Cross
  • Can help without an emergency being declared

ACS

Auxiliary Communications

Spokane County Emergency Management's own volunteer communications unit.

Field marks

  • Has passed a county background check
  • Can be activated by the county
  • Works inside emergency operations centers, incident command posts, hospitals and shelters

ACS carries on the county's RACES program (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service), the FCC provision that lets hams operate for government during emergencies.

You can join ARES only, or both. The two sides train and operate together, and our welcome page puts it plainly: Even [if] you don't or can't qualify for ACS, our ARES group welcomes you.

Backup communications for the county

When Spokane County Emergency Management activates the group, members staff the county's radio communications room and comm trailer. They pass messages when normal systems are overloaded or down, or where no links exist at all.

Hospitals

A hospital team keeps backup radio and computer equipment in place and working at Greater Spokane area hospitals. A Hospital Net follows the regular net.

Community events

Primary or backup communications for Bloomsday, the Lilac Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bicycle races.

SKYWARN weather spotting

Many members are trained spotters for the National Weather Service in Spokane, and nets activate in severe weather. Spotting is encouraged, not required.

Digital messaging

Winlink sends email-style messages by radio. The group runs the W7GBU-10 Winlink gateway, and members practice with assignments every two weeks.

Drills and exercises

Weekly nets, workshops and exercises like the annual Simulated Emergency Test keep skills fresh.

Licensing in plain English

The amateur radio exam works like the written test for a driver's license, with one big difference: every question in the pool is published in advance.

You can practice the real questions for free, take a local class if you'd like a teacher, then sit the exam with volunteer examiners. To join, you need a Technician license or higher.

  1. Practice

    Drill the published questions until they feel familiar.

    Practice free on HamStudy

  2. Take a class

    Classes run through the year, sponsored by Spokane County Emergency Management, the VHF Club and local ARES.

    Ask about the next class

  3. Pass the exam

    Volunteer examiners give the test, often right after a class. The FCC charges a $35 application fee.

Why W7GBU?

Our call sign and repeater honor Ira "Dean" Bula, who started the morning round table known as the 7:30 Bunch.

"He was a person who made a difference in Spokane by making others feel like they could make a difference themselves."
