What do these settings mean?

Just listening? Tune to 147.300 MHz. That's all you need.

Transmitting? Your radio sends on 147.900 MHz (the +600 kHz offset) with a 100.0 Hz tone, so the W7GBU repeater relays you across the county.

Every Tuesday night we practice. The day it's real, we're ready.

Spokane County ARES-ACS is one team of licensed amateur radio operators who volunteer to provide backup communications when normal systems are overloaded or down.

The Tuesday net is our weekly drill. One net control station runs the frequency and calls each group in turn, the same way an emergency net runs. Net control rotates among members, and the Net Manager coaches anyone taking the chair for the first time.

Members check in at least once a month, and weekly is encouraged. Visitors are always invited.

“Amateur radio can be particularly helpful because it can function even if other modes of communication cannot.”

Eastern Washington Standing Order 1b

How to check in as a visitor

A Tuesday net runs in this order. Visitors come after members.

  1. 1

    Officials

    Net control opens with the preamble and takes officials first.

  2. 2

    Members, by call-sign suffix

    Net control calls three groups in turn.

    • A–FAlpha–Foxtrot
    • G–MGolf–Mike
    • N–ZNovember–Zulu
  3. 3

    Visitors: your turn

    When net control asks for visitors, key up and give your call sign, spoken phonetically. Then wait for net control to acknowledge you.

    Not licensed yet? Listen along and hear how it works.

  4. 4

    Simplex and cross-band stations

    Stations not on the repeater are picked up last.

Two sides of one team

You can join ARES only, or both. The two sides train and operate together.

ARESAmateur Radio Emergency Service

A national ARRL program open to any licensed ham. ARES members can support community events, the National Weather Service and the Red Cross without an emergency being declared.

Who can join: anyone with an FCC Technician license or higher. ARRL membership is not required.

ACSAuxiliary Communications

Spokane County Emergency Management's own volunteer communications unit, carrying on the county's RACES program. ACS members have passed a county background check, so the county can activate them to work inside emergency operations centers, incident command posts, hospitals and shelters.

Who can join: ARES members who complete the county volunteer application and background check.

In the group's own words: Even [if] you don't or can't qualify for ACS, our ARES group welcomes you, and you can still volunteer for many events.

Emergency operations
When Spokane County Emergency Management activates us, members staff the county radio room and comm trailer.
Hospitals
Backup radio and computer equipment at Greater Spokane area hospitals, and a Hospital Net that follows the regular net on some Tuesdays.
Public events
Primary or backup communications for Bloomsday, the Lilac Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bicycle races.
Severe weather
SKYWARN spotting for the National Weather Service in Spokane. Nets activate in severe weather.
Digital messaging
The W7GBU-10 Winlink gateway, and regular Winlink assignments on ICS-213 forms.
Exercises
The annual Simulated Emergency Test, statewide exercises and joint drills with partner agencies.

Four conditions, and a plan for each one

The current condition sits at the top of every page. Here's what members do at each level, from a quiet week to a full deployment.

  1. Now, as of update date

    Green

    Inactive

    Normal conditions.

    MembersDo a monthly battery check.

  2. Yellow

    Alert

    “Something might happen.”

    MembersCheck go-kits and monitor frequencies.

  3. Orange

    Stand by!

    “Something has happened.”

    MembersLoad the car. Monitor NWS WXL86 on 162.400 and W7GBU on 147.300.

  4. Red

    Deploy!

    Activation text sent. Full nets start.

    MembersCheck in with net control on W7GBU, 147.300.

After the event: members are released, the condition returns to Green, and an action review is held within 72 hours.
