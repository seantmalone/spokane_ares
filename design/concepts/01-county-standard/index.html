One team, two ways to serve

Spokane County ARES-ACS is one team of licensed amateur radio ("ham") operators who volunteer to provide backup communications when normal systems are overloaded or down.

ARES is a national ARRL program open to any licensed ham. ACS is Spokane County Emergency Management's own volunteer communications unit, carrying on the county's RACES program. You can join ARES only, or both. The two sides train and operate together.

What we do

"Amateur radio can be particularly helpful because it can function even if other modes of communication cannot."

Eastern Washington Standing Order 1b

  • Emergency communications for the county

    When Spokane County Emergency Management activates us, members staff the county's radio communications room and comm trailer.

  • Hospital backup

    A hospital coordinator works with Greater Spokane area hospitals to keep backup radio and computer equipment in place and working. A Hospital Net follows the regular net.

  • Public service events

    Primary or backup communications for Bloomsday, the Lilac Torchlight Parade, fun runs and bicycle races.

  • SKYWARN weather spotting

    Many members are trained spotters for the National Weather Service in Spokane, and nets are activated in severe weather.

  • Digital messaging and Winlink

    The group runs the W7GBU-10 Winlink gateway. Members practice with biweekly Winlink assignments on ICS-213 message forms.

  • Training and exercises

    A directed net every Tuesday, Second Saturday workshops, Third Thursday meetings, and exercises such as the annual Simulated Emergency Test.

How a call-out works

Members track four readiness levels. Each one says exactly what to do next. For agency partners

  1. Condition Green

    Inactive

    Normal conditions. Check your batteries once a month.

    Current level

  2. Condition Yellow

    Alert

    Something might happen. Check your go-kit and monitor frequencies.

  3. Condition Orange

    Stand by

    Something has happened. Load the car and monitor NWS WXL86 (162.400) and W7GBU (147.300).

  4. Condition Red

    Deploy

    Activation text sent and full nets start. Check in with net control on W7GBU (147.300).

After the event, members are released, the level returns to Green, and an action review is held within 72 hours.

Your path to joining

Start wherever you are. Steps 0 to 2 make you an ARES member; step 3 is optional and lets Spokane County call on you directly.

  1. 0

    Step 0: Get licensed Your first step

    You need an FCC Technician class license or higher. License classes run periodically through the year, sponsored by Spokane County Emergency Management, the VHF Club and local ARES-RACES.

    License classes and exam sessions

    Listen first

    Listening needs no license. Tune a scanner or radio to the Tuesday net at 8:00 PM on 147.300 MHz to hear how the team works.

    Take a class Confirm schedule

    Classes are typically led by AD7FO with volunteer examiners, and testing follows the class. Ask about the next one through the contact form.

    Take the exam Confirm details

    • Washington Digital Radio Enthusiasts (WA7DRE), Laurel VEC: usually the third Wednesday, 6:00 PM, at Action Recycling, 1027 E Marietta Ave. No volunteer fee; the FCC charges $35.
    • Kootenai Amateur Radio Society: 2nd Monday (not December), 5:30 PM, at Kootenai County OEM in Hayden, Idaho. $15.
    • Online sessions through GLAARG on HamStudy.

    Help now, without a license

    SKYWARN spotter training from the National Weather Service is offered in person or online.

    Ask about the next class
  2. 1

    Step 1: Join ARES Your first step

    Fill out the Spokane County ARES registration form and bring it to a meeting or email it in. Download the ARRL ARES Task Book to track your participation and qualifications.

    Register for ARES ARRL ARES Task Book
  3. 2

    Step 2: Get connected

    Subscribe to the group's Groups.io list for meeting news. Check in to the Tuesday net, and come to a Third Thursday meeting or a Second Saturday workshop.

    Join the Groups.io list See the calendar
  4. 3

    Step 3: Qualify for ACS Optional

    Submit the county's Volunteer Emergency Worker Application and pass a background check. Then attend New Member Orientation, usually the 4th Tuesday at 6:00 PM, followed by a comm room tour and the net. New members work toward the Radio Operator level over about a year.

    Our primary served agency. It activates ACS, runs background checks and issues volunteer IDs.

    SKYWARN spotting and severe-weather nets.

    Support under the agreement between ARRL and the Red Cross, plus shelter training.

    A formal agreement covering the 146.88 backup repeater and packet and Winlink nodes.
