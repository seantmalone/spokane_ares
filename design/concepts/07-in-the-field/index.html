Behind every message is a volunteer with a radio.

Spokane County ARES-ACS is a team of licensed amateur radio operators who provide backup communications for Spokane County Emergency Management, the National Weather Service, local hospitals and community events.

Raising an antenna mast in freezing fog during a field exercise.

Backup communications, run by trained volunteers.

When phones, radio systems or the internet are overloaded or down, Spokane County still needs messages to get through. Our members are licensed amateur radio (“ham”) operators who volunteer to carry them, and who practice on the air every Tuesday night.

ARES

Amateur Radio Emergency Service

A national ARRL program open to any licensed ham. ARES members support community events, the National Weather Service and the Red Cross without an emergency being declared.

ACS

Auxiliary Communications

Spokane County Emergency Management's own volunteer communications unit. It carries on the county's RACES program (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service). ACS members have passed a county background check, so the county can activate them to work in emergency operations centers, command posts, hospitals and shelters.

Join ARES only, or both. The two sides train and operate together. In the group's own words: “Even [if] you don't or can't qualify for ACS, our ARES group welcomes you, and you can still volunteer for many events.”

3 of 5Emergency management

Inside the trailer.

When Spokane County Emergency Management activates the team, members staff the county's Radio Communications room and comm trailer. That work is for ACS members, who have passed a county background check.

On activation, members get a text, full nets start, and everyone checks in with net control on W7GBU, 147.300 MHz.

4 of 5Digital messaging

Anywhere with a battery.

A radio, a laptop and a battery make a message center. Through the group's W7GBU-10 Winlink gateway, members send email-style messages over the air, and keep in practice with biweekly Winlink assignments on ICS-213 forms. Station gear should run for at least 72 hours.

5 of 5Severe weather

When the sky turns.

Many members are trained SKYWARN spotters for the National Weather Service in Spokane, and the group activates nets in severe weather. Spotter training is offered in person or online, and you can take it before you are licensed.

This role: SKYWARN spotter

From curious to on the air.

Four steps. Start at the one that fits you. There are no membership dues, and you bring your own radio; there are no specific equipment requirements.

  1. 0

    Get licensedIf you don't have a call sign yet

    You need an FCC Technician license or higher. License classes run through the year, sponsored by Spokane County Emergency Management, the VHF Club and local ARES. You can listen to the Tuesday net before you're licensed.

    License classes and exam sessionsOnline exam sessions (GLAARG)
  2. 1

    Join ARESOpen to any licensed ham

    Fill out the Spokane County ARES registration form and bring it to a meeting or email it in. Download the ARRL ARES Task Book to track your training. ARRL membership is not required.

    ARES registration form (PDF)ARRL ARES Task Book
  3. 2

    Get connectedMeet the team

    Join the Groups.io list, check in to the Tuesday 8:00 PM net as a visitor, and come to a Third Thursday training meeting or a Second Saturday workshop.

    Join the Groups.io listMeeting schedule
  4. 3

    Qualify for ACSOptional: lets the county call on you

    Apply as a county Volunteer Emergency Worker and pass the background check. Then attend New Member Orientation and complete FEMA IS-100, 200, 700 and 800 (free online courses).

    County volunteer applicationFull ACS Task Book
