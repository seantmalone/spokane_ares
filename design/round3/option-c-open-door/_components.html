What the alert levels mean

Members follow four readiness levels. They describe what to do. They are not a public status, and this site never shows a current level.

  1. Green Inactive

    • Normal amateur radio operations.
    • Monthly battery check and recharge.

  2. Yellow Alert

    • Check radios, go-kits and supplies.
    • Watch National Weather Service statements.

  3. Orange Stand by

    • Load the car, including your go-kits.
    • Monitor WXL86 (162.400 MHz) and W7GBU (147.300 MHz).

  4. Red Deploy

    • Check in with net control on W7GBU.
    • Go to your assignment or a staging area.

Leaders hold an action review within 72 hours of release.

Activation in five steps

  1. Something might happenCheck your kits; monitor.
  2. Something has happenedLoad the car; monitor W7GBU.
  3. The county activatesAn activation message goes out.
  4. Check inWith net control on W7GBU, then go to your post.
  5. Release and reviewAction review within 72 hours.

How a message moves

A scenario. Not a real event.

  1. The phones go quiet

    At a warming shelter, the manager needs to reach the county’s Emergency Operations Center.

  2. A volunteer picks up the radio

    The ACS member writes an ICS-213 and reads it to net control on W7GBU.

  3. The radio room copies it

    Another volunteer copies it word for word and logs it on an ICS-309.

  4. The EOC acts on it

    Through the agency’s own channels. Every hop is on paper.

Questions newcomers ask

“Do I need my own radio?”

Not to visit. To listen, any radio or scanner that receives 147.300 MHz works.

“I’m not licensed. Can I still come?”

Yes. Anyone can listen to the net, and visitors are welcome at meetings and workshops.
