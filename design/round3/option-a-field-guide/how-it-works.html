How it works

Emergency radio works only when people practice it, so most of what we do is practice.

The weekly net

A net is an on-air meeting. Ours is a directed net: net control calls each group in turn, so everyone gets heard.

Every Tuesday, 8:00 PM

W7GBU
147.300 MHz+600 kHz100 Hz tone
Alternate
146.880 MHz−600 kHz123 Hz tone

What happens, in order

  1. 1

    Net control opens the net; emergency or priority traffic may break in at any time.

  2. 2

    Officials check in.

  3. 3

    Members check in.

  4. 4

    Visitors check in.

  5. 5

    Simplex, no-tone and cross-band stations, then relays.

  6. 6

    Traffic and late check-ins, then net control closes the net.

Visiting?Wait for the call for visitors. Give your call sign slowly and phonetically, then your name and location. Not licensed? Just listen.

Other nets

  • Winlink nights: 2nd and 4th Tuesdays; net control gives a Winlink assignment during the net.
  • Fifth Tuesdays: the net starts on simplex, then moves to W7GBU.
  • ACS GMRS net: 3rd Tuesdays, 7:30 PM, for county volunteers with GMRS licenses.

When the county calls

The county activates ACS members; ARES members serve events and the National Weather Service without a call-out. ARES and ACS

It has happened for real: in the Gray and Oregon Road fires of August 2023, the group provided a mobile network unit for the incident command post and the Disaster Assistance Center.

  1. 1

    Request. The county needs radio support.

  2. 2

    Mission number. The county gets a state mission number, which ties the activation to Washington’s emergency-worker protections.

  3. 3

    Activation message. It goes to members registered for notices.

  4. 4

    Check in. Available members check in with net control on W7GBU.

  5. 5

    Assignment. Go to your assignment or a staging area.

  6. 6

    Release. Served agencies and the emergency coordinator release members.

  7. 7

    Review. Once every station is secured and home, leaders hold an action review within 72 hours.

When no one has called yet

If communications fail and there are no instructions, the Eastern Washington Section’s Standing Order 1b applies. Safety comes first: no one is asked to leave home or travel. Then send a Washington State Field Situation Report by Winlink.

Forms and logs

The ICS-213 is the Incident Command System’s standard message form. It travels by voice, on paper or inside Winlink. Keep it short: To, From, Subject, Date, Time, the message and who approved it. The reply goes on the same form.

Precedence: Emergency, then Priority, Welfare and Routine.

  • ICS-309 communications log: every message sent or received. Winlink Express can build it.
  • ICS-214 activity log: what you did and when.

Winlink

Winlink is email by radio, with forms and attachments. It works with or without the internet. State and USGS forms are built in; reports can be plotted on a map. The group runs its own gateway, W7GBU-10.

Training and exercises

  • Every month: a workshop and a training meeting.
  • October: the Simulated Emergency Test, the Great ShakeOut and the State COMMEX.
  • With partners: joint exercises with other agencies, and EOC-to-EOC exercises across the county.
  • On the air: ARRL Field Day, Winter Field Day and SKYWARN Recognition Day.
