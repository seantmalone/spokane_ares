ARES and ACS

ARESAmateur Radio Emergency Service
The ARRL’s volunteer program, open to any licensed ham.
ACSAuxiliary Communications
Spokane County Emergency Management’s volunteer radio unit, open to ARES members who pass a Sheriff’s Office background check.
RACES
The FCC’s rule for amateurs serving government (97.407). The county’s RACES program is now part of ACS, so older documents say “ARES/RACES.”
AUXCOMM
The federal qualification ACS is modeled on, not a separate group to join.

What we are not

  • Not first responders. We support the agencies that respond.
  • Not a replacement for 911 or the county’s radio systems.
  • Not a public alert service. For alerts, register with ALERT Spokane (opens spokanecounty.gov).
  • Not a source of official incident information.

What this means for you. Protection comes from being a registered county emergency worker (the ACS path) on an activity with a state mission number. ARES-only activities, such as community events, are not county activations.

Who we serve

  • Spokane County Emergency Management: primary; activates ACS.
  • National Weather Service, Spokane: SKYWARN.
  • Area hospitals: backup communications.
  • Community event organizers.

Agreements

  • Inland Empire VHF Radio Amateurs (IEVHFRA), MOU Revision 5 (May 6, 2023): a backup repeater, a second repeater for special nets, and packet paths into Winlink.
  • National, through the ARRL: FEMA (2023), American Red Cross (renewed 2021), NWS SKYWARN (1986).

Joining

  • No membership dues.
  • Members use their own radios, any brand. You need nothing to visit.

ARES

  1. 1

    Fill out the Spokane County ARES Membership Application (2024-11-07). It includes a county non-disclosure agreement.

    Get the ARES application

  2. 2

    Hand it to the Emergency Coordinator or an AEC at a meeting, or email it.

  3. 3

    Track your training in the ARRL ARES Task Book.

ARRL membership isn’t needed to join. The ARRL requires it from its Intermediate level up; EC and AEC appointments also need the Advanced level.

ACS (optional)

  1. 1

    Join ARES.

  2. 2

    Submit the county’s Volunteer Emergency Worker Application (opens spokanecounty.gov).

  3. 3

    Pass the Sheriff’s Office background check. The county assigns your volunteer number.

  4. 4

    Attend New Member Orientation. It’s held regularly; ask us for the next date.

  5. 5

    Serve as a New Member, normally up to a year, while you finish Level 1.

  6. 6

    Advance to Radio Operator and receive your ACS ID card.

Equipment

  • Personal kit: food, water, clothing, hygiene items and medication for 24 hours minimum, 72 preferred.
  • Radio kit: 2 m and 70 cm radios, spare batteries, antenna, coax and power for 24 to 72 hours. HF and Winlink encouraged.

Getting licensed

The Technician exam works like a driver’s written test, except every question in the pool is published in advance.

Three ways to study

  • On your own: free practice sites such as HamStudy.org (opens hamstudy.org) drill the real questions.
  • With a class: local license classes (opens ag7qp.com).
  • Online: ARRL study guides and video courses.

History and W7GBU

The W7GBU story

Our call sign honors Ira “Dean” Bula, W7GBU. For years he handed out technical wisdom and friendly advice from the counter at Northwest Electronics. Each morning he led an on-air round table on the repeater that now carries his call. Newly licensed hams found him there, and it became the 7:30 Bunch, which still meets weekday mornings. When the county retired the repeater’s old call sign, the Spokane County ARES-RACES Support Group applied for Dean’s call to inspire its members.

“He was a person who made a difference in Spokane by making others feel like they could make a difference themselves.”
