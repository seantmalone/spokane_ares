## ARES and ACS {#ares-acs}

<!-- Alias ids on this H2 (or empty spans at its top): #what-is-ares, #what-is-acs, #two-hats, #auxcomm. Put #what-we-are-not on the callout. -->

- **ARES**, the Amateur Radio Emergency Service: the ARRL's volunteer program, open to any licensed ham.
- **ACS**, Auxiliary Communications [verify]: Spokane County Emergency Management's volunteer radio unit, open to ARES members who pass a Sheriff's Office background check.
- **RACES** is the FCC's rule for amateurs serving government (97.407). The county's RACES program is now part of ACS, so older documents say "ARES/RACES."
- **AUXCOMM** is the federal qualification ACS is modeled on, not a separate group to join.

> **What we are not** {#what-we-are-not}
> - Not first responders. We support the agencies that respond.
> - Not a replacement for 911 or the county's radio systems.
> - Not a public alert service. For alerts, register with [ALERT Spokane ↗](https://www.spokanecounty.gov/3007/Alert-Spokane).
> - Not a source of official incident information.

## The legal basis, in plain English {#legal-basis}

<!-- Whole section data-verify="true": SCEM must review it before launch, and it must not overstate protection. -->

| Layer | Rule | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Federal | [47 CFR Part 97 ↗](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-97) | Every member holds an FCC license. |
| Federal | [97.407, RACES ↗](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-97/subpart-E/section-97.407) | Agency-certified operators, civil-defense traffic only, drills capped at one hour a week (more with approval). |
| State | [RCW 38.52 ↗](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=38.52) and [WAC 118-04 ↗](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=118-04) | Emergency-worker registration and coverage. |
| County | Spokane County emergency plan, 2021 | ACS supports ESF-2, the county's communications function. |

> **What this means for you.** Protection comes from being a registered county emergency worker (the ACS path) on an activity with a state mission number. ARES-only activities, such as community events, are not county activations. [verify]

## Joining {#membership}

- No membership dues. [verify]
- Members use their own radios, any brand. You need nothing to visit.

### ARES {#ares-path}

1. Fill out the Spokane County ARES Membership Application (2024-11-07). It includes a county non-disclosure agreement.
   [CTA: Get the ARES application -> members/documents.html#ares-application]
2. Hand it to the Emergency Coordinator or an AEC at a meeting, or [email it](mailto:join@spokares.org). [verify]
3. Track your training in the [ARRL ARES Task Book](#arrl-task-book).

ARRL membership isn't needed to join. The ARRL requires it from its Intermediate level up; EC and AEC appointments also need the Advanced level.

### ACS (optional) {#acs-path}

1. Join ARES.
2. Submit the county's [Volunteer Emergency Worker Application ↗](https://www.spokanecounty.gov/FormCenter/Emergency-Management-31/Volunteer-Emergency-Worker-Application-276).
3. Pass the Sheriff's Office background check. The county assigns your volunteer number.
4. Attend New Member Orientation. It's held regularly; ask us for the next date. [verify]
5. Serve as a New Member, normally up to a year, while you finish [Level 1](#acs-task-book).
6. Advance to Radio Operator and receive your ACS ID card.

## Training and task books {#training-path}

Task books list what each level requires. The Emergency Coordinator or any AEC signs off items; the EC signs off each level.

| Level | Once | Regularly |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · New Member | Orientation; FEMA IS-100.c, 200.c, 700.b, 800.d; ARRL Basic EmComm; HIPAA; ALERT Spokane | Net check-in monthly; meeting or workshop quarterly; field operation yearly; kits ready and handheld programmed yearly; ICS-309 log on every operation |
| 2 · Radio Operator (RADO) | Radio room and trailer competency; ARRL Intermediate EmComm | Net control yearly; ICS-214 log; First Aid/CPR every two years |
| 3 · Senior Radio Operator (S-RADO) | General license; set up the Mobile Network Units | Winlink over VHF yearly; lead or assist an event yearly |
| 4 · Leadership | FEMA IS-120, 230, 235, 240, 241, 242, 244, 288 | Radio support for an event or the county, yearly |

Higher levels keep up the New Member requirements. Encouraged: weekly check-ins, monthly meetings, SKYWARN training, the AUXCOMM course.

FEMA courses are free online: [course links](members/documents.html#training) · [ACS Task Book (DOCX)](members/documents.html#acs-task-book)

### ARRL ARES levels {#arrl-task-book}

They use the same courses: Basic = Level 1's FEMA and ARRL Basic courses; Intermediate adds ARRL Intermediate EmComm; Advanced = Level 4's FEMA courses, minus IS-120 and IS-235. [ARRL ARES Task Book (PDF) ↗](https://www.arrl.org/files/file/ARES%20Taskbook%20July%202024.pdf)

### Equipment {#equipment}

- **Personal kit:** food, water, clothing, hygiene items and medication for 24 hours minimum, 72 preferred.
- **Radio kit:** 2 m and 70 cm radios, spare batteries, antenna, coax and power for 24 to 72 hours. HF and Winlink encouraged.
- [Go-kit guide](members/documents.html#go-kits)

## Getting licensed {#licensing}

The Technician exam works like a driver's written test, except every question in the pool is published in advance.

**Three ways to study**

- **On your own:** free practice sites such as [HamStudy.org ↗](https://hamstudy.org/) drill the real questions.
- **With a class:** [local license classes ↗](https://ag7qp.com/licenses).
- **Online:** ARRL study guides and video courses.

**Where to take the exam** [verify]

| Option | When | Where | Exam fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| After a local class | End of class day | Class location | $15 |
| Washington Digital Radio Enthusiasts (Laurel VEC) | Usually 3rd Wednesday, 6:00 PM | Action Recycling, 1027 E Marietta Ave, Spokane | None |
| Kootenai Amateur Radio Society | 2nd Monday, 5:30 PM (not December) | Kootenai County OEM, 1662 W Wyoming Ave, Hayden, ID | $15 |
| Online (GLAARG) | Many sessions | Book through HamStudy | Varies |

- **FCC fee:** $35 for a new license, on top of the exam fee.
- **Bring:** photo ID, your FCC Registration Number (FRN, get it before exam day), and your license if upgrading.
- **Under 18:** the ARRL VEC exam fee is $5, and the ARRL reimburses the FCC fee.
- **Before you're licensed:** [listen](how-it-works.html#nets), [visit](index.html#visit), or take [SKYWARN training](members/documents.html#skywarn-training).

## History and W7GBU {#history}

### The W7GBU story {#w7gbu}

Our call sign honors Ira "Dean" Bula, W7GBU. For years he handed out technical wisdom and friendly advice from the counter at Northwest Electronics. Each morning he led an on-air round table on the repeater that now carries his call. Newly licensed hams found him there, and it became the 7:30 Bunch, which still meets weekday mornings. [verify] When the county retired the repeater's old call sign, the Spokane County ARES-RACES Support Group applied for Dean's call to inspire its members.

> "He was a person who made a difference in Spokane by making others feel like they could make a difference themselves."

**Timeline**

- **1999:** SKYWARN Recognition Day station at NWS Spokane, every year since. [verify]
- **2004:** The earliest website records show the Tuesday net and Third Thursday meeting.
- **2017:** The FCC grants W7GBU's current club license. Second Saturday workshops begin.
- **2019:** The county sets up ACS.
- **2026:** New net scripts and a new website.
