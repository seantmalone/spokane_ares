# Homepage concept review: Spokane County ARES-ACS

Reviewed 25 September 2026. This covers twelve working homepage concepts for a full redesign of spokares.org. Each one is a separate bet on how to get a visitor to learn more and then sign up, while staying professional and easy to use. None of them is bound by the current site.

- Gallery to browse the concepts: `design/concepts/index.html` (open it in a browser; every screenshot links to the live concept)
- Concept specs: `design/concept-specs.md`
- Concept pages: `design/concepts/NN-slug/index.html`

## How the concepts were judged

Four judges reviewed every concept, each through one lens:

| Judge | Lens |
|---|---|
| Prospective volunteer | Newly licensed and unlicensed residents. Would I learn more, and would I sign up? |
| Served-agency partner | County EM, hospital coordinator, Red Cross liaison. Is this credible enough to link to? |
| UX and maintainability | Task speed for all four audiences, accessibility, and whether volunteers can keep it current. |
| Art director | Craft, distinctiveness, memorability, and fit for an emergency-services volunteer group. |

Each judge scored every concept from 1 to 10 on six criteria: professional, engaging, ease of use, makes you want to learn more, makes you want to sign up, and overall. The criterion scores below are the mean of the four judges, rounded to one decimal. The composite is the mean of the six criterion means, and it sets the rank.

### Each judge's top pick

No two judges picked the same concept.

- **Prospective volunteer: Field Guide.** It serves both the newly licensed and the unlicensed. Next came Open Door, Lines Down and Hi-Vis, all on 8.
- **Served-agency partner: County Standard.** It is the only concept with an explicit agency door and a coordinator contact, and it explains RCW 38.52 and state mission numbers. Coverage and Lines Down tied behind it on 8.
- **UX and maintainability: Open Door.** It best balances all four audiences, and the "This week" card serves members and newcomers at once. County Standard and Plain Signal came next.
- **Art director: When the Lines Go Down.** Nothing else makes the value of the work felt so strongly. Coverage was a very close second and is the lower-risk choice.

## Concept summaries

### 1. Field Guide (09): composite 8.21

*Thesis:* Most people have no mental picture of emergency radio. Friendly technical illustrations and a two-minute "Is this for me?" quiz give them one, then route each person to the right first step.

- **What works.** All four judges praised Fig. 1, the hero diagram (handheld, W7GBU repeater, county EOC): it explains the system in five seconds, and the agency judge would show it to a commissioner. The five-question quiz works and routes each person to one first step: license, ARES, ACS or SKYWARN. "Licensing in plain English" (the driver's-test analogy, published questions, the $35 fee, local exam sites) handles the unlicensed audience better than any other concept. It has no visible placeholders, and the mobile layout holds up.
- **Concerns.** The Caveat handwriting, rounded cards and cartoon figures read as edtech or a children's museum, too soft for a unit that works inside EOCs. Real members appear only as small taped polaroids. Four cards crowd the hero, and the quiz appears twice. There is no agency contact path, and member tasks are thin. The custom SVG illustrations (about 32 KB) are hard for volunteers to extend.

### 2. When the Lines Go Down (12): composite 7.92

*Thesis:* Walk the reader through one plausible ice-storm night and show how a message still gets from a shelter to the EOC by radio. Then reveal that the people who make it work practice every Tuesday.

- **What works.** It makes the strongest case for why the work matters. A scenario, clearly labelled as one, follows a single ICS-213 from a warming shelter, through net control on W7GBU, to the EOC radio room and the hospital net. Each hop is named as a volunteer role, so readers can see what they would do. Agencies see real procedure (ICS-213, ICS-309). The scroll from night into dawn ends on "Radio still works. Because people practice.", which leads straight into the Tuesday net. It has a "Skip the story" link and a reduced-motion version.
- **Concerns.** The join path starts about 6,000 px down, with long empty dark stretches between story steps, and members have to skip the story on every visit. The fear-first opening needs a PIO check, because a screenshot could circulate as a real alert. Scrollytelling is brittle for a non-developer to edit. The practical second half loses energy, and there are few real photos.

### 3. Open Door (04): composite 7.83

*Thesis:* The easiest yes is "come visit". Lead with three no-commitment doors (listen to the net, drop into a meeting, join a workshop), and membership follows belonging.

- **What works.** It makes the lowest-friction ask in the set, "Come say hello on Tuesday night", backed by a "This week" card that serves every audience at once: net details, net control, the next three events and member quick links. "Three ways to visit" removes first-visit anxiety better than anything else here. It gives a minute-by-minute run of the net, an at-a-glance box (how long, where, what to bring, whether you need a license), add-to-calendar and an optional RSVP. The Atkinson Hyperlegible body text, strong contrast and correct mobile order earned the UX judge's top score. The "Training with partners this fall" row is good evidence for agencies.
- **Concerns.** The hero is about socialising, not capability, so an emergency manager lands on a club greeting. The peach card looks dated and off-brand. The agency judge counted more visible "verify" and "to confirm" chips here than in any other concept, and there is a public "Condition Green" badge in the hero. Weekly data needs one data file or calendar feed, and the RSVP needs a mail handler with spam protection. The two hero buttons compete at equal weight. The page is long (about 10,800 px) and repeats itself in places.

### 4. Coverage (11): composite 7.54

*Thesis:* Local is the hook. Put Spokane County itself on the homepage as a calm interactive map of where the team works.

- **What works.** It is the most distinctive and locally grounded concept. A hillshade topographic map has layer toggles and pins, and each pin opens a panel naming the role it needs, with Join and Listen buttons. To agencies it reads like a county GIS product. It handles sensitive sites with care: hospitals appear only as hatched areas, and staging sites are members-only. The isometric County, ARES and ACS layer diagram explains the two-program structure more clearly than anything else in the set. It includes a short interest form.
- **Concerns.** It is the hardest concept to maintain: about 580 lines of procedural map JavaScript and hand-set pin coordinates. "Illustrative" disclaimers weaken the authority the map is meant to project. "Is my home in reach?" implies a coverage prediction the group can't stand behind, and residents could read it as a promise of service to their household. On mobile the map fills the first screen before the headline. Above the fold it shows places, not people.

### 5. In the Field (07): composite 7.42

*Thesis:* Show the work, not the gear. Real members in real conditions, with every scene linked to the role a visitor could play there.

- **What works.** It has documentary black-and-white photography and a strong line: "Behind every message is a volunteer with a radio." The "Find yourself in the frame" hotspots tie each photo to a role and its training. "Seven places you could stand" gives the best answer in the set to "what would I actually do?". The closing join form is short.
- **Concerns.** It depends on a photo shoot that hasn't happened. "Photo to commission" boxes are visible, and the 659 px source photos look soft at full width. The all-black palette feels somber, and at about 11,700 px it is the longest page. Member information is weak. Hotspots must be repositioned whenever a photo changes. Hospital or EOC photos would need releases and a HIPAA and OPSEC review.

### 6. Hi-Vis (03): composite 7.33

*Thesis:* Treat joining as a challenge worth taking, with an honest, numbered commitment and a county volunteer ID as the goal.

- **What works.** It has the most motivating voice and the highest sign-up score (8.8). "Here's what it takes" (1 net check-in a month, 1 meeting a quarter, 1 field operation a year, a 72-hour go-kit) is the most honest statement of commitment in the set. "Pick your post" lists seven roles with eligibility tags. The four steps to a volunteer ID have checkboxes that update a sample ID card. The page is mostly static and easy to edit.
- **Concerns.** The all-caps condensed type and safety yellow read like a sports, military or campaign poster, which could put off agencies and older hams (professional 6.0). The halftone treatment hides members' faces. The Dean Bula memorial quote in the hero confuses newcomers. Net information is missing above the fold, and "Verify" tags are visible.

### 7. County Standard (01): composite 7.13

*Thesis:* Credibility is what converts. Read like a well-run public service, name the county in the first line, and give each audience a clear door.

- **What works.** It has the highest professional score (9.0). It names Spokane County Emergency Management in the first line and offers four audience doors under "Which describes you?". It is the only concept with an explicit agency door, "Contact the emergency coordinator" and an Agreements link. Its ARES vs ACS comparison table is the best explanation of the two programs for partners. It is mostly static HTML with about 59 lines of JavaScript, so a volunteer can maintain it on shared hosting.
- **Concerns.** It scored 4.8 on engaging: a generic civic template with a boilerplate headline and little feeling. The top strip copies the USWDS "official government website" banner and should not imply official status. It shows a public "Condition Green" badge. "Join the team" leads to a list of forms with no quick way to say "I'm interested".

### 8. Tuesday, 2000 Hours (05): composite 7.08

*Thesis:* The Tuesday net is the heartbeat, so make the live schedule the hero and let a curious ham listen tonight.

- **What works.** A licensed ham can act tonight. The countdown is computed from the recurring schedule, so it maintains itself. The page has a radio-settings grid with "Copy radio settings", a visitor check-in walk-through, and an action button on every join step. The headline, "When other systems go down, Spokane County still has a voice", is excellent. It has the broadest partner list, with marks noted as pending permission.
- **Concerns.** The dark console look reads as insider tech (the art judge compared it to a crypto dashboard), and unlicensed visitors meet offsets and tones before any reason to care. The countdown mostly reads "03 days". The site-wide "Condition Green: normal operations" bar is the most serious stale-status risk in the set. Weekly items still need hand edits.

### 9. Plain Signal (06): composite 6.63

*Thesis:* Make the homepage itself the joining sequence, 0 to 3, with nothing decorative.

- **What works.** It has the highest ease-of-use score (9.3) and is the shortest page (about 5,400 px). The "Where are you starting from?" control reorders the steps and produces a shareable `?from=` link that can be read out on the net. The 0 to 3 numerals come with "How long" and "You need" columns. Mobile and accessibility are excellent, and it is the easiest concept to keep up.
- **Concerns.** It has the lowest engaging score (3.5): no logo in the header, one photo, no people and no story. It can read as a stub or a template, and it is the weakest at making people want to learn more. The served-agency line is set in muted gray, and "to be confirmed" strings are visible.

### 10. The Relay (02): composite 6.54

*Thesis:* People sign up for a story they believe in. A well-edited magazine feature, with every acronym explained in the margin.

- **What works.** It has the best headline in the set: "From Bloomsday to blackouts, we keep Spokane County connected." The margin glossary defines every acronym and becomes tap-to-define on mobile, which is the best answer to jargon in the set. It states its scope clearly ("nobody here becomes a first responder"). A net strip stays on every page, and prose is easy to edit.
- **Concerns.** It asks for a three-minute read before the pitch. The join path sits about 7,000 px down, with only one Join button near the top. There is little for members, and agencies have to dig for capability. The light display serif is thin, and "[Photo credit TBD]" placeholders are visible.

### 11. Confirmed Contact (08): composite 6.29

*Thesis:* Borrow the QSL card, ham radio's ritual for "you're in", and turn each first step into a card you collect.

- **What works.** It is the most ham-native idea. The hero QSL card carries the real net details, and the "Cards to collect" path has a progress tracker. "Get your card from W7GBU" builds a personalised, shareable QSL. The W7GBU and Dean Bula heritage gives the group a soul.
- **Concerns.** The metaphor only works for insiders. Unlicensed visitors and agencies don't know what a QSL card is. The agency judge scored it 3 and would not link to it: it reads as a hobby club, and gamified collecting trivialises a readiness role. It carries about 300 lines of JavaScript (canvas plus localStorage) and 15 "verify" or TBD markers, and the sign-up is spread across five cards.

### 12. Ops Board (10): composite 5.92

*Thesis:* A site members open every week stays alive. Build the homepage as a calm operations board with a permanent recruiting rail.

- **What works.** It is the best member portal: left-rail navigation, search, a next-net card with the check-in order, a net-control roster with "Take this slot", the Winlink assignment, and a task book listing the FEMA IS courses. To agencies it shows an organised, NIMS-aligned unit. The sticky Join and Listen bar on mobile is a smart touch.
- **Concerns.** To newcomers it looks like an internal app, and the recruiting rail is visually secondary. It carries the highest maintenance burden in the set, and a stale board visibly damages credibility; an "updated [time]" placeholder is showing. The offline toggle and search are mock-ups that need real development. The art judge's verdict: it belongs at /members, not on the homepage.

## Where the judges agree

- **The message-path diagram from When the Lines Go Down should be in the final site.** All four judges included it in their suggested hybrid. The art judge wanted it as the homepage opener; the other three wanted it as a section or a "How it works" page.
- **Public status indicators are a liability.** "Condition Green" badges in 01, 04, 05 and 10 would contradict official county messaging if they went stale during a real incident. Drop them from public headers, label them as internal team readiness, or make them expire.
- **Weekly data needs one source.** Net control, simplex weeks and the coming-up list appear in most concepts. One editable data file (JSON or a PHP include) or a Groups.io calendar feed should drive them all, or the site will go stale.
- **Content gaps are visible.** The "verify", "TBD", "[Photo credit TBD]" and "Photo to commission" chips must be cleared before any partner sees a page. The county seal and ARRL emblem need written permission (05, 11 and 12 already note this).
- **The photo library is the weakest asset.** Every concept draws on the same five or six photos, most of them low resolution. Any direction benefits from a photo shoot, and hospital or EOC scenes need releases and an OPSEC review.
- **The accessibility baseline is sound everywhere.** All twelve have a skip link, a main landmark, an accessible mobile menu toggle and alt text, and they handle reduced motion. None supports dark mode. The concepts differ on type choices and interaction complexity.

## Recommendation

### Carry forward three directions

1. **Field Guide, as the newcomer front door.** It ranked first and best serves the audience the site most needs to grow: people who have never pictured emergency radio. Keep Fig. 1, the quiz and "Licensing in plain English". Fix its tone for agencies: replace the Caveat handwriting with a sober annotation style, thin out the four competing hero cards, show real members at a useful size, and add an agency door.
2. **Open Door, as the structure and usability base.** It was the UX judge's top pick and the prospect judge's second. Its "This week" card and "Three ways to visit" module are the most practical pieces in the set for both members and first-time visitors. Keep Atkinson Hyperlegible. Feed every weekly item from one data file, replace the peach card, add a capability line to the hero, and stop the two hero buttons competing at equal weight.
3. **When the Lines Go Down, as the "How it works" story.** Keep its ICS-213 message path as a condensed homepage section and a full "How it works" page, not as the whole homepage, so the sign-up path is never 6,000 px away. Get a PIO review of the scenario copy and check it against county shelter and EOC plans.

A practical round two is one hybrid homepage: Open Door's skeleton and "This week" card, Field Guide's Fig. 1 and quiz in a more grown-up visual register, and a short Lines Down section linking to the full story. If partner credibility matters more than recruiting reach, the alternative is County Standard's structure with the Lines Down section and a light Coverage map, which is what the agency judge proposed.

### Graft in from lower-ranked concepts

- **County Standard (7):** the ARES vs ACS comparison table, the "I represent an agency" door with "Contact the emergency coordinator" and Agreements, the one-line statement naming Spokane County Emergency Management (without the government-banner styling), and the footer organised by audience.
- **Hi-Vis (6):** the "Here's what it takes" commitment block (1 net a month, 1 meeting a quarter, 1 field operation a year, a 72-hour go-kit) and "Pick your post" with eligibility tags, restyled in sentence case.
- **Plain Signal (9):** the 0 to 3 join path with "How long" and "You need" columns, and the shareable `?from=` link.
- **Coverage (4):** a lightweight, static county map for "Where we work" or "Who we serve", keeping its handling of sensitive sites, plus the short interest form with its "where are you on the route" choice. Drop the ZIP lookup.
- **Tuesday, 2000 Hours (8):** the self-computing next-net line, "Copy radio settings", and an action button on every join step.
- **In the Field (5):** "Seven places you could stand" as an alternative to "Pick your post", once a photo shoot has happened.
- **The Relay (10):** tap-to-define glossary terms for acronyms, and the headline "From Bloomsday to blackouts, we keep Spokane County connected."
- **Ops Board (12):** move it to /members as the member portal.
- **Confirmed Contact (11):** the personalised "Get your card from W7GBU" QSL as a reward for newly licensed hams, and the W7GBU heritage story for an About page. Keep both off the homepage.

### Fix before any direction ships

- Remove or relabel public "Condition Green" status.
- Put all weekly data behind one data file or calendar feed.
- Clear every review chip and placeholder, and secure permission for the county seal and the ARRL emblem.
- Commission a photo shoot, with releases and an OPSEC review for anything shot at a hospital or the EOC.
